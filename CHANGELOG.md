# Changelog

All notable changes to this project. Language policy: English throughout [DD-30].

## [0.25.0.dev0] — unreleased

Pre-release audit. Changes that alter results are listed first.

**Added**

- **Meta-recurrence [DD-119]:** `meta_space`, `meta_recurrence` (RP, CRP and
  JRP of the per-window measurements), `meta_theiler`, and
  `WindowedRecurrence.meta_recurrence()`. The Theiler window excludes
  overlapping windows automatically.

- **Figures that explain a recurrence plot [DD-120]:** `plot_recurrence_panel`
  (RP, CRP, JRP or meta-RP with its trajectories in the margins and,
  optionally, its RQA measures), `plot_joint_recurrence` (each subsystem, the
  conjunction and a coloured overlay) and `plot_rqa_summary` (the line-length
  distributions behind DET and LAM). A gallery in the README, regenerated by
  `tools/make_gallery.py`.
- **Licence:** MIT, in `LICENSE` and `pyproject.toml`; `CITATION.cff` for
  GitHub's citation button. The logo is in `docs/_static/`.

**Usability [DD-118]** (old spellings keep working)

- `threshold()` defaults to `theiler=1`, like the plots; a plot built from a
  `Threshold` inherits its Theiler window and warns if given a different
  window or metric.
- `store="stream"` replaces `store="none"` (still accepted).
- The generators accept `rng` as well as `seed`; `mvl` accepts `normalise`.
- `windowed_recurrence`, `windowed_cross_recurrence` and
  `windowed_joint_recurrence` list `target_rr`, `theiler`, `metric`, `store`
  and `precision` explicitly; `precision` now reaches windowed plots, and
  `cross_recurrence_plot` has it too.
- `threshold()` warns when epsilon falls to rounding level.

**Changed (results move)**

- **The Theiler window follows the standard convention [DD-115].**
  `theiler=w` now excludes `|i - j| < w`, as in the CRP Toolbox, pyunicorn
  and PyRQA; it excluded `|i - j| <= w` before. `theiler=1` removes the line
  of identity only. To reproduce an earlier result, pass `theiler=w + 1`.
  Applied to the matrix, the rate denominator, both line histograms, the
  white-line band and the pair sampler; neighbour searches (FAN, geometry
  descriptors, D2, K2, Lyapunov) use `|i - j| >= max(theiler, 1)`. DD-19 is
  marked revised.
- At `theiler=0` the threshold sampler now keeps self-pairs, matching the
  matrix, which keeps its line of identity.
- **Cosine recurrence plots are correct [DD-110 revised].** The coordinates
  were centred before every kernel, which is exact for Euclidean, Chebyshev
  and Manhattan and wrong for cosine: on an offset point cloud at a target
  rate of 0.05 the matrix came out at 0.0013. Only translation-invariant
  metrics are centred now; all four are checked against `scipy` cell by cell.
- **Classical indices refuse a rescaled amplitude [DD-116].** In the windowed
  table, a z-scored amplitude gave MVL 13.3 and MI 0.88. A rescaled amplitude
  block now yields NaN for MVL, MI and the modulogram summaries, with one
  `CaveatWarning`; `mvl`, `modulation_index` and `modulogram` warn when the
  amplitude has negative values, and MI returns NaN when a phase bin's mean is
  negative. `CoordGroup.params` records `scale` and `smooth_hz` per block.
- **The fine-scale decimation keeps at least 64 samples [DD-99 revised]**,
  and warns when the cap acts. It left nine samples on a 250-point window.
- **Envelope smoothing preserves the sign [DD-117].** The default smoother is
  now a Gaussian kernel with gain 1/2 at the cut-off, as the Butterworth had.
  The Butterworth rang below zero on strongly modulated envelopes (4.7% of
  samples at alpha 0.9). The Gaussian rolls off more gently, so it keeps less
  of the modulation just below the cut-off: place the cut-off at about 1.5
  times the phase band's upper edge. `smooth_method="butterworth"` reproduces
  earlier results.

**Fixed**

- **D2 could lock onto the saturating plateau of the correlation sum [DD-121].**
  On a Lorenz trajectory 1e-9 away from the test's, it returned 1.61 against
  2.05, unflagged. The automatic search now stays where `C(r) <= max_C`
  (5% by default). Found through the first GitHub CI run, which failed on the
  macOS (ARM) runners for the same test.
- `dynamics_measures` given a plain 1-D array computed every measure on its
  first sample only (`atleast_2d(x).T[0]`), so all of them failed.
- `modulogram` did not wrap the phase: a phase in [0, 2π) put every value
  above π in the last bin. `modulation_index` always wrapped it.
- **FNN and Cao picked exact duplicates as nearest neighbours.** On a sine
  sampled at an integer number of samples per period (or on quantised EEG),
  FNN counted every duplicate as a false neighbour and returned the top of
  `m_range` (12 for a circle). Both now use the nearest *distinct* neighbour,
  after collapsing points closer than 1e-9 of the attractor size; Lorenz still
  gives m=3 and tau=16, a sine now gives m=2.
- **The declared floor `numpy>=1.24` did not work.** `spawn_rngs` read
  `bit_generator.seed_seq`, public only from numpy 1.25, so every parallel or
  multi-stream path failed at 1.24. Found by running the suite at the floors
  (Python 3.10, numpy 1.24, scipy 1.10, pandas 1.5) for the first time.
- `estimate_tau` and `estimate_m` warn when the estimate is the edge of the
  search range or the criterion was never met, instead of returning it
  silently (P3).
- `recurra generate` wrote the ground-truth table and discarded the signals it
  had generated. It now writes each signal under `<out>/signals/`.
- `examples/run_all.py --out DIR` did not redirect anything, because every
  example fixed its own output directory. They now honour
  `RECURRA_EXAMPLES_OUT`, which `run_all.py` sets.

- `tests/test_docs.py` read the changelog in the platform's default encoding,
  which fails on a Windows code page for characters such as subscripts.

**Tests**

- The slow validation tests pin the conditions their findings were measured
  under, `l_min = v_min = 2` and the pre-0.25 Theiler window, in one place at
  the top of `tests/test_validation.py`. Seven of them had failed since DD-98
  moved the defaults to 8.
- Three Lyapunov tests asserted facts about one chaotic trajectory, which
  differs between scipy versions: two passed on Python 3.12 and failed on 3.14
  with identical code. They are replaced by two contracts checked on three
  trajectories: an unflagged exponent lies within 25% of the reference.
- The Lyapunov contract test passed its contract (every unflagged estimate
  within 25%, no bad value unflagged) and failed a second clause that assumed
  `max_steps=300` is always sound; on one trajectory it gave 1.273. The
  clause now asks only that the guard does not flag everything, and DD-101
  records the measured specificity: two of four good estimates were flagged.
- A regression test for the windowed options: `target_rr`, `theiler` and
  `metric` set to non-default values must reach every window's threshold.
  An intermediate draft of this version dropped them silently (DD-118).
- New tests for the command line (it had none) and for embedding estimation:
  AMI against an independent histogram estimate, Lorenz recovered, an exactly
  periodic series, warnings at the edge of the search range.
- New fast tests: every distance metric against `scipy`; the exact Theiler
  band at four windows and two tile sizes; FAN never counting a point as its
  own neighbour; classical indices on a rescaled amplitude; the modulogram's
  phase wrapping; the fine-scale cap.

**Documentation**

- DD-98 now carries the `v_min` revision it announced; DD-99, DD-101 and
  DD-110 carry revision notes; DD-115 and DD-116 are new.
- This changelog: the 0.15.0.dev0 entry was an exact copy of 0.22.0.dev0's
  and now says so; the three 0.7.3.dev0 entries are one entry with three
  passes.
- The last four Spanish words in docstrings ("memoria") are translated.

## [0.24.0] — 2026-09-20

Consolidation release. One line of code changes with respect to 0.24.0.dev0
(below). The 514 fast test cases pass; nine of the 31 slow ones fail, exactly
as they did on 0.24.0.dev0 (see Known issues). The documentation is brought
into step with what the code already did.

**Fixed**
- `dynamics/invariants.py` used `Sequence` in an annotation without importing
  it. Harmless at run time because annotations are postponed, but
  `typing.get_type_hints(invariants_from_series)` raised `NameError`

**Known issues** (pre-existing in 0.24.0.dev0, measured here, not fixed)

The fast suite (514 cases) passes. Of the 31 slow cases, nine fail on Linux
with numpy 2.4.4, scipy 1.17.1 and pandas 3.0.2: eight in `test_validation.py`
and one in `test_dynamics.py`. The same nine fail on 0.24.0.dev0; the slow
suite was run on both. Being slow, these tests were not re-run when the
decisions that broke them landed. Three causes:

- Seven tests in `test_validation.py` re-derive findings that were measured with
  line floors of 2 (DD-65, DD-67, DD-70, DD-77) but call `rqa()` with its
  defaults, which DD-98 moved to 8 in 0.22. In a synthetic space laminar
  states are short, so LAM at `v_min=8` is near zero and carries no
  information (AUC 0.500, one division by zero). Run with `l_min=2, v_min=2`
  injected, 27 of the 28 slow validation tests pass. The findings stand; the
  tests must pin the floors they were measured at
- `test_every_measure_is_computable_on_a_short_window`: on a 250-point window
  the fine-scale decimation of DD-99 leaves 9 samples, and every fractal and
  entropy measure of both blocks fails as too short. A library behaviour gap:
  the decimation needs a floor on the samples it leaves
- `test_the_lyapunov_exponent_is_averaged_over_samplings` (DD-101) compares the
  spread of four single-sampling estimates with four averaged ones and is
  decided by chance (SD 0.054 against 0.088). Over twelve seeds averaging
  narrows it modestly, SD 0.138 to 0.119, means 0.932 and 0.928 against 0.906.
  The test is underpowered and DD-101 overstates the gain

Proposed fixes in `docs/ROADMAP.md`. CI runs the slow tests, so until they
land the first push is red.

**Changed**
- `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`: the module map no longer lists `parallel/` twice,
  once as done and once as pending; section 7 is the current roadmap instead
  of the F7 plan written before parallelism and the real-data readers landed;
  the ground-truth footnote no longer lists the Lorenz invariants as pending
- `docs/INSTALL.md` quotes the real test count
- `README.md`: the status line no longer announces as next what is already
  implemented, and links the new roadmap

**Added**
- `docs/ROADMAP.md`: what is specified and not written, what the first real
  study needed and wrote outside the library, and the release checklist

## [0.24.0.dev0] — unreleased (superseded by 0.24.0)

**Fixed**
- a cross-validation test fold holding a single class is now recorded with a
  `degenerate_test` flag and NaN scores instead of being scored: sklearn's
  balanced accuracy warned "y_pred contains classes not in y_true" there and
  returned a degenerate number. Its predictions still enter the repeat-level
  concatenation, which always holds both classes, so the reported AUCs are
  unchanged. A repeat whose usable folds only ever tested one class is
  skipped rather than scored

**Added** [DD-109]
- `classify_groups`, `classify_timecourse` and `group_timecourse` take
  `n_jobs` for their permutation loops (and, in the timecourse, for the
  aligned windows). The default 1 keeps them serial — right when the caller
  already parallelises across channels; shuffles are seeded by position or
  drawn up front, so every p-value is identical at any worker count [DD-81],
  and two tests pin `n_jobs=1` against `n_jobs=2`

## [0.23.0.dev0] — unreleased

### Correctness review: five things measured and changed

**Fixed** [DD-110]
- The distance kernel now centres the coordinates before expanding the norm.
  In single precision an offset of 1000 in every coordinate moved the
  recurrence rate from 0.051 to 0.082 and flipped 5.6% of the cells; the
  expanded norm loses a few parts in 10^7 of the *norm*, not of the distance.
  A phase-amplitude space is nearly centred and was not affected; a raw channel
  with a DC offset would have been. Offsets of 100, 1000 and 10 000 now leave a
  single-precision plot bit for bit unchanged, and a test says so. DD-79 is
  marked revised

**Changed** [DD-111]
- `ENTR_norm` and `RTE` divide by log of the number of *admissible* lengths,
  `Lmax - l_min + 1`, instead of log of the number of lengths *present*. The
  old denominator moved by one whenever a single line of a new length
  appeared. **Values of these two columns are not comparable across this
  version boundary**; raw `ENTR` is unchanged

**Added** [DD-112]
- `pac_space` warns when `smooth` cuts below the upper edge of the phase band.
  A cut-off inside the band removes the modulation before it is measured;
  8 Hz was right for theta and blind to alpha and beta
- `ingest(meta={"bands": {name: (lo, hi)}})` is the documented way to hand the
  band edges over when the decomposition was done outside the library, so
  that guard -- and the slow/fast auto-selection -- work on precomputed data

**Added** [DD-114]
- `group_timecourse`: per-window AUC curves between two groups on aligned
  windows, with subject-label permutations giving a max-statistic `p_peak`
  (corrected for taking the best window) and a `p_trend` for drift along the
  recording. Quality-masked rows stay excluded under every permutation
- `classify_timecourse`: one honestly cross-validated classification per
  aligned window (folds split by subject, learning inside the fold), the
  classifier counterpart of the same curve, with optional whole-curve
  permutation

**Added** [DD-113]
- `pac_space` warns when the amplitude band is narrower than twice the phase
  band's upper edge: the sidebands of the modulation do not fit and the
  band-pass removes the coupling before the envelope exists. Measured: a
  35-45 Hz band around a 40 Hz response gives MVL 0.014 for a theta
  modulation that a 30-50 Hz band reads at 0.203

**Changed** [DD-98]
- `WindowedRecurrence.metrics()` defaults `l_min` and `v_min` to 8, matching
  `rqa()`. They had stayed at 2 after the top-level default moved, so the two
  entry points returned different DET for the same matrix. A test pins them to
  each other

**Fixed**
- the `"classical"` feature family now matches the column names
  `coupling_indices` actually writes (`mvl`, `mi_tort`, `mod_contrast`); it
  only knew the uppercase spellings and could not find the library's own
  output. `preferred_phase` is circular and stays out of the linear features
- `dynamics_measures` no longer turns a failing scalar measure into a silent
  nan; it warns with the exception, as the invariants already did [DD-95]
- The phase-increment series a phase-circle block contributes [DD-71] is built
  with central differences and no longer starts with an artificial zero
- `sample_entropy` counts `N - m` templates for both `m` and `m + 1`, as
  Richman and Moorman define it, and records in its diagnostics when the
  series was truncated to `max_points`
- `design_bandpass`: the four-second cap on the FIR length now warns when it
  binds and reports the cycles actually delivered; a leftover `len(range(0))`
  is gone
- `lyapunov_max`: a duplicated block of the degenerate-neighbour check removed
- `subsample` states in its caveat that no anti-alias filter is applied and at
  which frequency content folds back; part of what DD-80 measured at factor 5
  is the gamma circle aliasing, not the metrics reacting to sparser sampling
- `rqa(denominator="all")` is documented as restoring the line of identity
  only: with `theiler > 1` the rest of the band was never evaluated
- `plv` documents which phase `n` multiplies

## [0.22.0.dev0] — released as 0.22.0.dev0

### Windowed metrics run in parallel

**Added** [DD-109]
- `BatchWindowedRecurrence.metrics(n_jobs=...)`. The build was already
  parallel; the metrics were not, and they are four fifths of the work --
  measured, 4.8 s to build a 19-window record against 20 s to walk it for RQA.
  On 49 subjects that was sixteen minutes per channel on one core
- The histograms DD-108 caches do not survive a process boundary: a worker
  returns a copy and its cache stays behind. They are handed back explicitly

### The windowed sweep, from milliseconds instead of minutes

**Added** [DD-108]
- `WindowedRecurrence.line_length_sweep()` and the batch equivalent: DET and
  LAM at several floors, per window, from the histograms the RQA pass already
  accumulated
- Before this the only route was calling `metrics()` once per floor, which
  invalidates its cache and re-walks every matrix. Measured on a 19-window
  batch: 50 s per floor against 13 s to build the batch, so a seven-floor
  sweep cost 350 s -- five times the analysis. Now 0.06 s
- It also silences the saturation warning, which was arriving once per floor
  per window through this path after DD-98 had removed it from the sweep

### v_min follows the evidence

**Changed** [DD-98]
- Default `v_min` moves from 2 to 8. The original 2 rested on a synthetic space
  where LAM's range went 0.71 at 2 to zero at 4; a real corpus of 49 delta-gamma
  recordings gave **LAM = 0.9983 at v_min=2**, saturated exactly as DET was.
  A synthetic envelope jumps between samples and a recorded one does not, so its
  laminar states are far shorter and a floor of 2 caught all of them
- The test now pins the departure from the classical 2 rather than the number:
  neither is right for every space, and `line_length_sweep` finds the right one

### Quieter diagnostics

**Fixed** [DD-98]
- `line_length_sweep` warned about saturation at every floor it evaluated,
  which is every floor it exists to evaluate -- once per floor, per subject,
  per channel. On a 31-channel run that buried every other message. The
  headroom column already reported the same fact as a number

### Surrogates, exported and characterised

**Fixed**
- `surrogate_significance` existed from the first phase and was reachable only
  as `recurra.metrics.classic.surrogate_significance`, which is not where
  anyone running a study looks. A 31-channel run failed on it. Exported, with
  a test that asserts the export

**Recorded** [DD-107]
- A circular-shift surrogate saturates its own z-score: the modulation index
  rose twentyfold from alpha 0.2 to 0.9 while z moved 2.04 to 2.61, because
  shifting preserves the envelope's own modulation and the null rises with the
  signal. Report the p-value; a z of 2.6 is the ceiling, not weak evidence
- The surrogate null of a realistic signal measured 0.0075 against 0.00002 for
  a smooth synthetic one. A real index compared against a synthetic baseline is
  overstated 375-fold

### The reference index, and the picture that shows coupling

**Added** [DD-106]
- `modulogram()` and `plot_modulogram()`: mean amplitude per phase bin, with
  the error bars. A scatter of the joint space cannot show coupling -- the
  spread within one phase bin is 4.4 times the movement of the mean across
  bins at a clearly detectable alpha of 0.3 -- and the conditional mean can:
  contrast 0.118 uncoupled, 0.888 at alpha 0.3, 2.546 at 0.9, with the peak on
  the true preferred phase
- `comodulogram()` and `plot_comodulogram()`: the coupling index for every
  band pair, which says *which* pair couples rather than assuming one. On a
  theta-to-gamma signal it puts that pair an order of magnitude above the rest
- `metrics(classical=True)` and `coupling_indices(..., shape=True)`: MVL, the
  modulation index, the modulogram contrast and the preferred phase, in the
  same table as DET and LAM
- 8 tests

**Why it matters**
- Forty-nine subjects and three channels had been analysed with 105 columns
  each and **no classical coupling index anywhere**. That is the difference
  between a null result and a well-measured absence: without it there is no way
  to tell whether the recurrence metrics are characterising a space that
  contains any coupling at all

### Figures normalised, and the polar view

**Fixed** [DD-105]
- Attractor figures on shared axes showed a blob, a ring and a full box for
  three control subjects whose attractors have the same shape. The state space
  was correct -- `tail_ratio_phase_theta` measured 1.407 to 1.420 across all 49
  subjects -- and what varied was epsilon, by a factor of eight
- `rms_balanced` fixes the *ratio* between blocks and leaves the absolute scale
  at `n / sum(1/rms^2)`, which follows the recording's gain. Measured over a
  thousandfold change in gain the circle radius went from 0.054 to 1.414 while
  DET moved 0.8%
- `shared_limits` and `plot_attractor` now normalise by it, and the axis labels
  say so. Nothing computed changes: the recurrence plot never used the absolute
  scale
- 6 tests

**Added**
- The polar view (`mode="torus"`) alongside the 3-D scatter in the study
  script: phase on the angle, amplitude on the radius, which is where coupling
  is legible by eye as an eccentric ring
- Panels split by group past a dozen subjects; a single figure with 49 3-D axes
  is unreadable

### Attractor figures, comparable at a glance

**Added** [DD-104]
- `shared_limits()` and `plot_attractor(..., axis_limits=)`: several figures
  written to separate files can now share their axes. The logic existed inside
  `compare_attractors` and nothing exposed it, so a caller writing one file per
  subject -- which is what examining thirty subjects across thirty-one channels
  means -- got a different scale in every picture
- All eight comparison figures are now exported at the top level. They existed
  only as `rc.viz.compare.something` while every single-object figure was
  reachable as `rc.something`; nothing intended that

### The tail as a column, and attractors as images

**Added** [DD-102]
- `shape_descriptors()`: `tail_ratio`, `skew` and `max_ratio` per coordinate
  block, and `geometry_descriptors` now includes them. The library has warned
  about heavy tails since DD-28 and never recorded how heavy, so nobody could
  check whether they differed between the groups being compared
- Measured with coupling held constant, the tail drove 22 of 39 metrics --
  spectral entropy at rho +0.93, Katz at -0.91, DET at +0.83 -- while every
  phase-block measure stayed below 0.3
- The warning's own advice was wrong: `robust` scaling changes nothing, because
  a linear rescaling cannot change the shape of a distribution. Only `rank` does

**Added** [DD-103]
- `attractor_image()` and `export_attractor_images()`: the trajectory as a
  fixed-size occupancy map, with the batch-shared scale, robust limits and the
  recorded projection that the recurrence images already had

**Verified**
- None of the feature changes since v0.13 touches the recurrence plot: matrix
  bit-for-bit identical, same epsilon, same state space, after exercising every
  changed knob. The line-length floors filter the line histogram; they do not
  decide which pairs are recurrent, and RR is identical at l_min 2 and 8



### The Lyapunov exponent, averaged rather than drawn

**Fixed** [DD-101]
- The divergence curve draws a few thousand reference points at random, and
  which ones were drawn was deciding the answer: twelve streams over identical
  Lorenz data gave 0.501 to 1.366 against a reference of 0.906, and **eleven of
  the twelve came back unflagged**
- `n_repeats` (default 3) averages over independent samplings and reports
  `sampling_cv`. Over eight runs the range narrowed from 0.380-0.903 to
  0.631-0.989 and the flag went from firing once to firing five times: the
  instability is now visible rather than silent
- DD-94's guard watches a different axis -- truncating the curve, not
  redrawing the points -- and neither replaces the other. Which dominates
  depends on the space: on a multivariate trajectory the curve length decides
  (1.13 at max_steps=150 against 0.894 at 400) and the sampling moves it by 4%
- A test that had been passing at max_steps=150 was fitting the transient and
  was corrected



### Measures that were reporting constants

**Fixed** [DD-99]
- Six of thirty-nine columns were constants across 98 real recordings --
  `petrosian_fd` at a coefficient of variation of 0.00003, `katz_fd` at
  0.00008 -- because the fine-grained measures were being asked about a scale
  where nothing happens. A gamma envelope low-passed at 8 Hz and sampled at
  250 Hz has 24 samples between changes of direction; white noise has 1.5
- `fine_scale="auto"` decimates each block to its own scale for those measures
  only, and records the factor and the ratio it came from. Higuchi goes from
  1.04 to 1.92, Lempel-Ziv from 0.17 to 0.92; DFA, Hjorth and the invariants
  are untouched to nine figures
- `samples_per_turn()` and `fine_scale_factor()` are public, because the
  diagnosis is useful on its own

**Fixed** [DD-100]
- The correlation dimension of a PAC space came out 1.9812 +- 0.0065 with
  R2 = 1.0000 on all 98 recordings. A perfect fit was the symptom: if the
  correlation sum is straight over its whole range there is no scaling region
  to find, and 2 is what a phase circle crossed with an amplitude gives
  whatever the signal does
- Flagged when the fit covers more than 90% of the curve with R2 above 0.9999;
  `region_share` is reported either way. Lorenz covers 54-57% and stays usable
  at 2.056 against a reference of 2.05

## [0.13.0.dev0] — unreleased

### Line-length floors, chosen from the data

**Changed** [DD-98]
- Default `l_min` is now **8**, `v_min` stays at **2**. On a real corpus DET
  came out 0.9965 +- 0.0005 at the classical floor of 2 -- a constant that
  cannot separate anything -- and 0.766 +- 0.047 at 8. Raising the vertical
  floor instead destroys LAM: its range went from 0.71 at 2 to 0.11 at 3 to
  exactly zero at 4. Treating the two as one number is wrong in both directions
- This departs from the literature and is recorded on every result; a DET
  computed here is not the DET of a paper that used 2
- The analytic ground truths now pass `l_min=2` explicitly, so a change of
  default cannot silently invalidate them

**Added** [DD-98]
- `line_length_sweep()`: DET and LAM at a range of floors, from one histogram.
  Every metric at every threshold is a sum over counts already accumulated, so
  choosing well costs milliseconds against the minutes the plot took
- The collapse case is now warned about as loudly as the saturation case. A
  metric crushed to zero separates nothing either, and the first version of the
  check only looked at the ceiling

## [0.12.0.dev0] — unreleased

### Invariants from a delay embedding, and two floors instead of one

**Fixed** [DD-95]
- `dynamics_measures` forwarded one option set to both `correlation_dimension`
  and `lyapunov_max`, which do not take the same options. Every block
  correlation dimension failed and, because the failure became a nan, the
  columns came out entirely empty across a 32-recording run

**Fixed** [DD-96]
- `theiler="auto"` for the invariants. Two points of a delay embedding closer
  than `(m-1)*tau` share coordinates; counting them as neighbours made a Lorenz
  embedding look one-dimensional. Measured: D2 of 1.60 at Theiler 1, 16, 32 and
  64, and 1.97 at 200
- The correlation sum reaches lower radii by default. At the 0.1st percentile
  the straight part spanned 0.39 decades and was rejected as too thin; at the
  0.02nd it spans 0.81
- Together: **D2 = 1.996 and lambda_1 = 1.009 on Lorenz, both unflagged**

### The Lyapunov diagnostic, corrected

**Fixed** [DD-94]

### Route comparison made honest, and a recommendation

**Fixed** [DD-53]

### Corrected the sign of the DD-02 hypothesis

**Changed**
- The hypothesis in DD-02 predicted that coupling would produce an *interior
  maximum* in the lambda sweep. The measured curves show the opposite: the
  **uncoupled** signal has the higher interior effective dimension, because
  independent blocks form a cartesian product whose dimension is the sum of
  the parts, while coupling is a constraint that collapses the joint space.
  The decision now records the wrong prediction, the measurement that
  contradicted it, and the corrected statement
- `lambda_sweep` renames its `*_synergy` columns to `*_deficit`; the previous
  name asserted the wrong sign
- Measured, same seed and spectrum, alpha 0 to 0.95: interior slope falls
  2.020 -> 1.452, Spearman(alpha, slope) = -0.943, consistent across three
  seeds to within 4%. Recorded with its two limitations: the effect is
  insensitive below alpha 0.6, and is uncontrolled for noise and record length
- Three tests fix the result: that the endpoints are uninformative, that
  coupling lowers the interior dimension, and that the column is named for a
  deficit
